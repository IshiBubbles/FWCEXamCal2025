**A recurring data hazard worth knowing about.** Search snippets on

independently and refused to attribute the contaminated strings. **These are exactly the
specifics that would get quoted into an application and then dismantled at interview** — so
where you see an attribution warning in an entry, take it seriously.

## WSP — does the local option actually involve site work?

## Priority answer — How much actual site time is in WSP's two Southampton civil degree apprenticeships?

**Short answer: neither is a site-engineer role. Building Structures has explicit, documented site content but it is subordinate to design work. Planning Development shows no documented site element at all and looks like the more desk-bound of the two. The honest position on *frequency* is that no source found quantifies it for either role — anyone telling Fraser "X days a week on site" for these two vacancies is guessing.**

#### What the two vacancy adverts actually say

**Building Structures — VAC1000300404 (advertised Southampton, snippet also says "Basingstoke or Southampton")**
The duties described in snippets of this vacancy are: working alongside a team of experts on local and national projects; designing structural frames and components for residential, commercial, public and industrial buildings in concrete, steel, masonry and timber; collaborating with architects and multidisciplinary teams; preparing technical documentation including drawings and calculations; ensuring compliance with building codes and client requirements; **and "attending site visits", described as coordinating with contractors and engineers and addressing design queries during construction.** `[paraphrased — snippet only]`

That last clause is the most useful sentence in the whole search. It confirms site visits are a *named duty*, not an aspiration. But note precisely what kind of site visit it is: the apprentice goes to site as the **designer answering the contractor's questions**, not as the contractor's engineer setting out, surveying, or supervising the works. That is a materially different activity from what Fraser means by boots-on-the-ground.

**Planning Development — VAC1000346490 (Southampton)**
Snippets describe: joining a high-performing team; **"supporting projects from land acquisition to construction"**; the team helping clients promote sustainable development, guiding plans from pre-feasibility through to planning consent and multidisciplinary design; and in the role, **"assist in design development and preparing technical documentation."** Permanent position, Monday–Friday, 37.5 hours/week. `[paraphrased — snippet only]`

Across four separate searches aimed specifically at this vacancy, **no snippet ever returned the words "site", "site visit", "survey" or "inspection" for the Planning Development role.** The phrase "from land acquisition to construction" describes the *project lifecycle the team supports*, not the apprentice's whereabouts. WSP's equivalent graduate scheme in the same business line — "Planning & Development (Civils Design)" — has its scope described as master planning, concept and detailed civil engineering design including highway design, earthworks design, surface water drainage design and foul water drainage design. `[paraphrased — snippet only]` That is a design office job description end to end.

**So yes, the two roles differ, and in the direction that matters to Fraser: Building Structures > Planning Development on site exposure.**

#### The internal WSP comparator that proves the point

WSP's **Guildford (Bridges)** civil engineering degree apprenticeship advert lists duties including bridge inspections using specialist access provision — pontoons, under-bridge inspection gantries, mobile elevated working platforms and confined-space entry techniques — organisation of site visits, structural design and assessment, design of structural repairs, supervision of contractors, and project management. `[paraphrased — snippet only]`

This is important two ways. First, it shows WSP genuinely does run field-heavy civil apprenticeships, so "WSP = desk job" is too crude. Second, it shows what a WSP advert looks like *when the role really is field-heavy* — and by that standard, neither Southampton advert reads as field-heavy. If site time is Fraser's binding constraint, **Guildford (Bridges) is the WSP role to watch, not the two Southampton ones** — and Guildford is a plausible-if-awkward commute or a modest relocation compared with Birmingham or Leeds.

#### What actual WSP apprentices say their days look like

- WSP "Undergraduate Engineer" reviews (RateMyApprenticeship / Higherin): day-to-day is **mostly design work in AutoCAD**, plus running small schemes — managing finances, **commissioning surveys** (i.e. ordering them, not walking them), managing C3s, dealing with clients and third parties, issuing permits. `[paraphrased — snippet only]`
- At least one WSP review snippet does mention attending site visits alongside one day a week at university. `[paraphrased — snippet only]`
- A TheJobCrowd snippet indicates that for **Transport** roles at WSP, typical time is around four days a week in the office, with field work, site visits and meetings making up roughly a fifth day. `[paraphrased — snippet only]` **Heavy caveat: that is Transport, not Building Structures or Planning Development, and it is a snippet-level statement, not a WSP policy.** Do not quote a "4 days office / 1 day site" split at interview as if it were WSP's published position — it would not survive being probed.
- Reviews are consistently positive on *support* (protected study time, flexibility around assignments, active tracking of apprentice progress) rather than on site variety. `[paraphrased — snippet only]`

#### Honest verdict

1. **Both roles are design-consultancy roles.** The core output is drawings, calculations, models and reports. That is what will fill most weeks.
2. **Building Structures is the better of the two for Fraser** — site visits are a named duty tied to live construction, so there is real, if occasional, exposure to a working site and to contractors.
3. **Planning Development is the weaker of the two for Fraser** — nothing found evidences a site component, and the sister graduate scheme is pure design.
4. **Frequency is genuinely unknown.** Nothing found states how often either apprentice goes to site. Realistically, for a consultancy design apprentice, expect *episodic* site attendance (tied to a project milestone or a construction query) rather than a routine weekly pattern — but that is an inference from the role type, not a sourced fact, and should be flagged as such.
5. **Best possible use of this:** Fraser can turn the uncertainty into a strong interview question. "How many site visits did last year's Southampton apprentice actually attend, and were they design-query visits or supervision?" is an excellent question — it demonstrates he has read both adverts, spotted the difference between them, and is thinking about the engineer he wants to become.
6. **Strategic honesty:** if boots-on-the-ground is non-negotiable, WSP Southampton is a *fallback that keeps him at home*, not his first choice. The genuine site-engineer route is a main contractor — and the trade Fraser faces is essentially "site work but commuting/relocating" versus "living at home but designing." He should decide that consciously rather than by default.

#### Timing — the most actionable WSP finding of all

WSP's 2026 degree apprenticeship applications ran **October 2025 to April 2026**, and snippets state applications **reopen in November 2026**. `[paraphrased — snippet only]` For a **September 2027** start that is exactly Fraser's window: **be ready to apply from early November 2026**, i.e. within weeks of starting Year 13. Both Southampton refs above are past-cycle adverts (a snippet explicitly states VAC1000300404 closed **Tuesday 1 April 2025**), so Fraser should expect the 2027-intake equivalents to be *re-advertised* from November 2026 — that re-advertisement is an inference from the stated cycle, not a confirmed fact, and the exact team names could change.
