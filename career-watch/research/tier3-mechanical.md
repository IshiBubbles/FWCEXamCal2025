**The clock.** Airbus's own UK apprenticeships page states that applications for apprenticeships at **Airbus Aircraft and Airbus Defence & Space starting in 2027 open in October**, and run **October until February**, with the explicit warning that *"applications may close earlier than expected — apply early to avoid disappointment"* `[paraphrased — snippet only, from airbus.com/en/careers/students-and-graduates/apprentices/apprenticeships-in-the-united-kingdom]`. That is **weeks from now**. Fraser should have a CV and covering letter drafted before the end of September 2026.

**The Portsmouth question — ANSWERED, and the answer is yes.** Airbus's own UK apprenticeships page says of the Portsmouth site: *Portsmouth is a key centre for engineering in space and intelligence systems, and with the team there you can complete a **Degree or Technical Apprenticeship** in fields such as systems and software engineering, project management, and electromechanical engineering* `[paraphrased — snippet only]`. **Also confirmed:** Airbus is running an **apprenticeship matching programme with the Solent Growth Partnership** — described as the UK's first space-and-defence apprenticeship matching scheme — which routes strong candidates who narrowly miss an Airbus place into opportunities with local SMEs `[paraphrased — snippet only]`. That materially reduces the downside of applying: a near-miss at Airbus Portsmouth is not a dead end.

## 🟡 The BAE window dispute — settled

**Verdict: applications open in EARLY NOVEMBER 2026 and close at the END OF FEBRUARY 2027. Treat "January 2027" as a mid-window date, not the opening. Apply in November.**

Evidence for early November opening and late-February closing:
- Search snippets state plainly: *"Applications for the 2027 intake will open in November"* and, separately, *"2026 applications opened early November 2025"* `[paraphrased — snippet only, attributed by the search engine to BAE's own apprenticeship FAQ page]`.
- The previous cycle is documented and consistent: Island Careers Partnership and STEM Hub both carried "BAE Systems 2026 Apprenticeships Now Open" notices, with **applications open until 28 February 2026** and **some roles expected to close early at midnight Friday 20 February 2026** `[paraphrased — snippet only]`.
- Independent confirmation from GOV.UK Find an Apprenticeship: BAE vacancy **V05085**, a Portsmouth Naval Base role with a **7 September 2026 start**, carried an **application deadline of Saturday 28 February 2026** `[verbatim date from findapprenticeship.service.gov.uk listing]`.

**How the "January 2027" claim probably arose.** Two plausible explanations, neither of which I can settle from snippets: either a page was read mid-cycle and reported the date it was viewed, or a specific business unit or scheme within BAE opens later than the group window. **The safe behaviour is identical either way — be ready to submit in the first week of November 2026.** Waiting for January risks arriving after roles have closed early, which demonstrably happened in the 2026 cycle.

## ⚠️ Cross-cutting finding: the Gradcracker "opening Autumn 2026" string is a template, not a date

Three of the eleven employers below (Leonardo, Amentum, Capgemini Engineering) returned an **identical** Gradcracker snippet pattern: the same discipline list — *"Aerospace, Chemical/Process, Civil/Building, Computing/Technology, Electronic/Electrical, Maths/Business, Mechanical/Manufacturing, and Science"* — followed by *"opening Autumn 2026."*

### Airbus (Airbus Defence & Space)
`Tier 1` · `mechanical` · `mixed` · **Portsmouth (Airbus Defence & Space), ~20 mi from Southampton; college element at CEMAST, Lee-on-the-Solent, ~15 mi**

**What they do.** Airbus is a European aerospace group with three divisions — commercial aircraft, helicopters, and Defence & Space — making money from aircraft sales and support, satellites and space systems, and long-run government defence contracts. **Airbus Defence and Space** is the relevant arm here: military and civil space, secure satellite communications, and intelligence systems. Its UK footprint spans Stevenage, Portsmouth, Filton, Broughton and Newport, and it is one of the largest space-sector employers in the country. Portsmouth is described on Airbus's own careers pages as a key centre for **engineering in space and intelligence systems** `[paraphrased — snippet only]`.

**What they're working on now.** Portsmouth's headline programme is **Skynet**, the UK MOD's sovereign military satellite communications system. The **communications module of Skynet 6A was built at the Portsmouth plant**, with the service module built at Stevenage, and the two were united for testing at the **National Satellite Test Facility**; **digital processors and payloads for next-generation military satellites including Skynet 6A are developed and integrated at Portsmouth** `[paraphrased — snippet only]`. Snippets indicate Skynet 6A testing continued through 2025 with **launch scheduled for 2026**, and that the **Future Protected Modem-A** is due to enter service in 2026 `[paraphrased — snippet only; treat both dates as needing confirmation, launch schedules slip constantly]`. There is also live local political activity: Portsmouth News reported pressure on the Government over the risk of Airbus Portsmouth being cut out of a future Skynet contract — worth knowing about, and worth *not* raising in a covering letter.

**Apprenticeship route.**
- **Degree Apprenticeship — Portsmouth site.** Airbus's own UK page names Portsmouth as offering a **Degree or Technical Apprenticeship in systems and software engineering, project management, and electromechanical engineering** `[paraphrased — snippet only]`. Exact programme titles and partner universities for the Portsmouth degree routes specifically were **not established** and need a browser check once the October window opens.
- **Degree-level entry requirements (Airbus-wide).** Airbus's published degree-level entry-requirements document states **Level 3 qualifications such as 3 A-levels at grades A–C, or a vocational equivalent, or 120 UCAS tariff points**, plus **minimum grade 4/C GCSE English Language and Maths** `[paraphrased — snippet only, from airbus.com degree_level_apprenticeships_entry_requirements.pdf]`. Note this is a **higher bar than the 104–112 UCAS points quoted on the Spacecraft Engineering route** — the two figures come from different documents and I am not reconciling them from snippets.
- **Technical Apprenticeship — Portsmouth site, 3 years.** *"Being a Technical Apprentice at Airbus Defence & Space means you will get to develop both mechanical and electrical skills within the three year programme"* `[verbatim, from snippet]`. Year 1 delivers **Performing Engineering Operations (PEO)** and a **BTEC National in Mechanical and Electrical Engineering**, then study towards an **HNC in Mechanical/Electrical Engineering, with the possibility of an HND** if eligible. Partner college: **CEMAST, Lee-on-the-Solent**. Entry: **at least five GCSEs including a grade 5/6 (B) in Maths and English** `[paraphrased — snippet only]`. **This is a Level 3→4 route, not degree level** — relevant only as a fallback.
- **Window:** opens **October 2026**, runs to February, may close early. **No salary figure was found for any Portsmouth route** — do not assume one.
- **Clearance/nationality:** the Spacecraft Engineering route required **eligibility for UK SC Security Clearance or a BPSS check**, and stated the roles **do not meet UK Visas & Immigration minimum requirements for sponsoring migrant workers** `[paraphrased — snippet only; note this string was attached to the Chippenham-located vacancy, and I am NOT asserting it is the wording used on Portsmouth vacancies]`.

**Stated criteria.**
- *"Being a Technical Apprentice at Airbus Defence & Space means you will get to develop both mechanical and electrical skills within the three year programme"* `[verbatim]`
- *"you may have been part of an engineering club at school or perhaps you enjoy fixing mechanical and electrical things in your spare time"* — offered as how to evidence passion for engineering `[verbatim]` — **this is the single most useful sentence in the entry for STAR purposes**
- Demonstrate a passion for engineering through educational choices or hobbies `[paraphrased — snippet only]`
- Ability to submit a CV with grades **and a covering letter stating motivations** `[paraphrased — snippet only, from the Spacecraft Engineering route]`
- Level 3: 3 A-levels A–C or 120 UCAS points; GCSE grade 4/C English Language and Maths `[paraphrased — snippet only]`
- Full person-spec bullets for a **Portsmouth degree** vacancy `[not found — needs a browser check; no Portsmouth degree vacancy text was retrievable]`
- **Residency: `[not found — needs a browser check]`.** Clearance eligibility (SC or BPSS) appears, but **no explicit years-of-UK-residency requirement was found in any Airbus snippet**. Do not assume BAE's residency wording applies to Airbus — that is exactly the cross-contamination this round is guarding against.

**Work setting.** **Mixed**, and honestly uncertain for the degree route. Evidence: the Portsmouth degree fields named are systems/software engineering, project management and electromechanical engineering — of those, only *electromechanical* clearly implies hands-on work, and satellite payload work happens in cleanrooms and integration halls rather than out in the field. The **Technical** route is unambiguously plant/workshop ("develop both mechanical and electrical skills", PEO qualification). If Fraser wants hands-on at Airbus, **electromechanical is the word to look for on the vacancy**.

**Why them — the hook.** The communications module of Skynet 6A — the satellite carrying the UK's sovereign military comms — was built at Portsmouth, and Portsmouth is where the digital processors and payloads for the next Skynet generation are developed.

**Fit for Fraser.** Strong, and the most time-critical thing on the list. Maths and Physics A-levels are directly on-target; Chemistry is neutral rather than helpful here. The realistic concern is *what he'd actually do*: Portsmouth's degree fields lean systems/software/project management, which is the desk risk. Electromechanical is the one to chase. The Solent Growth Partnership matching scheme means a near-miss still produces a local outcome, which makes an early October application close to a free option.
