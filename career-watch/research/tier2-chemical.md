## The two local options — and the finding that links them

The single most useful structural discovery in this round: **SETA is the training arm of the
ExxonMobil Fawley apprenticeship.** Fawley apprentices spend their first year at SETA Millbrook,
and ExxonMobil has funded SETA directly — a £6,000 donation, instrumentation equipment, and a
"Transfer to Transform" levy transfer. **These are not competing options. They are the same
route seen from two ends.**

That reframes the local question. Neither offers a Level 6 degree apprenticeship, but there is a
real ladder: the Fawley apprenticeship awards *"a level 3 BTEC qualification or a level 4 HNC,
depending on your previous qualifications"* — and three A-levels should put Fraser on the HNC
track rather than the BTEC one. SETA itself delivers HNC Level 4 and HND Level 5, with a
university top-up to Level 6 after that. So the honest read is that it under-uses three A-levels
at entry, but it is not a dead end, and it is ten miles from home.

### ExxonMobil Fawley
`Tier 1` · `chemical` · `plant` · **Fawley, Hampshire (~10–12 mi from Southampton, Hythe/Waterside side of Southampton Water). Other UK: Fife (Mossmorran, ~500 mi), Leatherhead/Midstream offices (~60 mi).**

## 2. SETA — Southampton Engineering Training Association

#### Specific questions answered first

**⚠️ Name collision warning, read this first.** There are **two unrelated organisations called SETA**. The Southampton one is at **`setatraining.co.uk`** (Millbrook, Southampton, SO15 0LJ). There is a separate **North-East England "Seta"** at **`seta.co.uk`** (Team Valley / Tyneside). Search results mix them constantly, and several apprenticeship-vacancy pages that look like Southampton results are actually the North-East body. **Anything sourced from `seta.co.uk` is not the Southampton organisation and has been excluded below.** Fraser must not fill in the wrong interest form.

**Q1. Which member employers actually take SETA apprentices, and are any local/notable?**
This is the key question and the honest answer is: **I confirmed the mechanism and a few named employers, but I could not obtain a member list.** What is established:
- SETA is a **not-for-profit registered charity (Charity Commission no. 307299)**, founded **1967/1969** (both dates appear in different snippets) specifically to train engineering apprentices for local industry, with a **Board of Trustees drawn from local engineering companies**. So the "members" are literally local employers who sit on its board — but the board/member list did not surface.
- It has **over 250 apprentices on programme** at any time.
- **The most notable and most local confirmed employer is ExxonMobil Fawley.** This is confirmed from both directions: SETA's own news pages carry ExxonMobil items, and the Fawley material states that "ExxonMobil Fawley apprentices study at Southampton Engineering Training Association (SETA) in Millbrook" for their Level 3 advanced apprenticeship, with **year one based at SETA**. ExxonMobil has also donated to SETA (£6,000) and supported it through the **"Transfer to Transform"** levy-transfer scheme. **This is the single most useful finding in this entry: SETA is the training arm of the Fawley apprenticeship.**
- Other named employers SETA was recruiting *for*, from 2026 vacancy snippets on `setatraining.co.uk` and GOV.UK: **Murray Technical Services Limited** (Level 3 Engineering Fitter (electrical) apprentice, control systems), **SMC Group** (Apprentice Engineering Technician, production team, manufacturing production qualification), and **Ingenieur Ltd**.
- A further list — **the Met Office, Cyclife Aquila Nuclear, The Score Group, Williams Engineering, IME Repair Services, BAT** — surfaced in one aggregated snippet about "SETA" employers. **I am refusing to attribute these to Southampton SETA.** The same snippet set was contaminated with `seta.co.uk` (North East) results, and several of those names (Score Group, Cyclife Aquila) are strongly North-East/Scotland-weighted. The Met Office would be geographically plausible (Exeter) but I have no clean source. **Treat all six as unverified.**

**Q2. Does completing a SETA L3/L4 ladder into a degree apprenticeship with a member employer?**
**No degree apprenticeship was found — but a clear non-apprenticeship academic ladder was, delivered by SETA itself.** SETA runs **HNC Level 4 General Engineering** and an **HND (Level 5)** in General Engineering in its own right. The stated progression is: **Level 3 apprenticeship → Level 4 HNC (two years part-time) → Level 5 HND → Level 6 engineering degree studied at university.** Snippets also state "many SETA apprentices go on to study HNCs, HNDs or degrees after their apprenticeship," and that a Level 3 BTEC Diploma in Engineering **at merit or distinction** is the entry to HNC. **What this is:** a genuine, evidenced route to a degree. **What it is not:** a degree apprenticeship. It is part-time study bolted on after/alongside employment, dependent on an employer agreeing to fund and release him, over roughly 3 (apprenticeship) + 2 (HNC) + 2 (HND) + top-up years. That is a long road compared with a five-year Level 6 degree apprenticeship somewhere else.

**Q3. Is the Sept 2027 interest form the right entry point, and when do vacancies appear?**
**Yes, it is the right entry point, and the timing is well evidenced.** SETA runs a "register your interest" form per intake year and confirms a **September 2027** one is live. The mechanism, from the 2026 cycle:
- **"From February 2026, the employers that SETA supports will steadily be going live with their vacancies for engineering apprenticeships that will be starting in September 2026."**
- SETA also says **"some employers start to advertise their vacancies as early as January or February to commence in September."**
- SETA hosted an **Engineering Apprenticeship Jobs Fair on 10 February 2026**, where "some of the employers that SETA works with will be there with their live or imminent vacancies."
So the realistic 2027 shape is: **register interest now (Aug 2026 is early and that is fine), expect a jobs fair around early February 2027, expect employer vacancies to go live from roughly January–February 2027 and trickle through spring, for a September 2027 start.** Critically: **SETA does not employ the apprentice — the member employer does.** The interest form gets him on the list; he still applies to individual employers. Recruitment contact that surfaced: **023 8077 1908 / recruitment@seta-training.co.uk** (note the hyphenated email domain versus the unhyphenated website domain — worth verifying).

**Q4. Honest read: reasonable route for someone holding three A-levels, or does it under-use them?**
**It under-uses them, and I would not pretend otherwise — but with one large caveat and one tactical use.**
The under-use is real. SETA's provision tops out at Level 4/5; the entry expectation for its apprenticeships is GCSE-level; it explicitly does not offer Level 6. Someone with A-level Maths, Physics and Chemistry is academically over-specified for a Level 3 engineering fitter or machining apprenticeship, and the two years of A-level Maths and Physics content will overlap heavily with the first part of an HNC he then studies part-time. If Fraser's ambition is chartered-engineer status, this route reaches it late and via more of his own time.
The caveat: **SETA's most notable client is the closest and best hands-on employer on the entire list.** The Fawley apprenticeship *is* delivered through SETA. So "SETA" and "ExxonMobil Fawley" are not two competing options — applying to Fawley means training at SETA. That reframes SETA from "a lesser option" to "the delivery mechanism for the best local plant job."
The tactical use: register the September 2027 interest form and attend the February 2027 jobs fair **regardless**, because it costs almost nothing and it is the only channel that surfaces small local employers who never advertise nationally. Use it as a **breadth-and-fallback channel** running underneath the degree-apprenticeship applications, not as the primary plan. And note the honest strategic risk: taking a Level 3 at 18 with three A-levels is a decision that is hard to reverse, whereas a Level 6 degree apprenticeship elsewhere delivers both the degree and the site time.
